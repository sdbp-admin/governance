# SDBP Working Constitution

**Status: draft for discussion — not adopted**

## Article 1. Purpose and foundations

SDBP develops and promotes economic relations between the Netherlands, including neighbouring regions in Belgium and Germany, and Slovenia, including the Adriatic region. It connects people, competences and business opportunities through international networking, constructive dialogue and cooperation.

This Constitution establishes SDBP’s fundamental organisational authority: how authority is distributed, how roles and circles function, how governance changes the organisation, how objections protect affected responsibilities, and how distributed authority relates to the formal responsibilities established by the Statutes and applicable law.

SDBP works with trust, ethical conduct, respect for different cultures, transparency and a willingness to share knowledge. These values guide conduct but do not themselves create organisational authority.

This Constitution operates alongside the SDBP Statutes and applicable law. Where the Statutes or law reserve authority or responsibility to the Board, General Assembly or another formal body, that authority remains with that body.

No provision of this Constitution transfers an authority that cannot legally or statutorily be delegated.

This Constitution applies to everyone exercising organisational authority or carrying work on behalf of SDBP.

---

## Article 2. Roles, circles and authority

A person and the roles they hold are distinct.

Organisational authority is exercised through roles, circles, governance and the formal bodies established by the Statutes.

Seniority, influence, expertise, access to information, historical involvement, personal relationships or informal leadership do not by themselves create organisational authority.

A role has a defined purpose and may include scope, responsibilities, accountabilities and decision-making authority.

An accountability describes something the role is expected to continue taking care of. Authority describes what the role may decide or do without requiring further permission.

Scope identifies the area in which a role operates. Scope does not create exclusive control unless this has been explicitly established.

Within its authority, a role may act independently unless that authority has been explicitly constrained or reserved elsewhere.

A circle is a continuing organisational unit with a defined purpose and mandate. It may contain roles and other circles.

A project is temporary operational work. A project does not become a circle merely because it continues for an extended period.

A Standing Agreement is a continuing organisational rule adopted through governance. Standing Agreements may grant, constrain or clarify organisational authority or establish an ongoing way of working within the authority available to governance.

Board membership does not create general managerial authority over other roles. Board authority arises from the responsibilities and powers attached to the Board under the Statutes, applicable law and this Constitution.

---

## Article 3. Roles and people

Everyone can participate in governance to create, change or remove non-statutory roles and circles.

People who are part of SDBP may choose or be chosen to fulfil roles and may step out of them. Taking or leaving a role is separate from hiring or firing a person.

The Board has authority to hire people into roles and to remove them from their positions when needed. Other role holders do not have this authority, and these decisions are not made by a vote of all governance participants.

Firing a person is a Board meeting decision. Minutes are mandatory and must be uploaded to the Workspace.

Creating, changing or removing a role or circle is a governance proposal. Removing a role does not by itself fire its holder or terminate membership, employment, contractual rights or another underlying legal relationship.

Assignment to a role does not make the role or its authority the personal property of the role holder.

Appointment and removal of Board members and admission or ending of SDBP membership follow the Statutes and applicable law.

## Article 4. Operational authority and tensions

Everyday operational decisions remain with the role or body authorised to make them.

Within their authority, role holders may organise their work, cooperate with others and take appropriate action without seeking additional permission.

A request from one person or role to another does not by itself create a commitment, transfer authority or change organisational responsibility.

A tension is a perceived gap between the present situation and a possible or needed situation.

A tension may concern a problem, opportunity, uncertainty, risk, conflict, unmet need or structural limitation. A person does not need to know the solution before raising one.

A tension is information. It is not itself a proposal, decision, accusation or objection.

A tension may be addressed through operational action, a request or commitment, direct conversation, governance, a formal statutory process, or no further action.

Operational work and the tension that gave rise to it remain distinct. Completing related work does not by itself establish that the original tension has been resolved.

Where an issue can be addressed within existing authority, it remains operational. Where it requires a continuing change to organisational authority, structure or Standing Agreements, it is a governance matter.

---

## Article 5. Governance

Governance changes SDBP’s continuing organisational structure and authority.

Through governance, SDBP may:

- create, amend or remove non-statutory roles and circles;
- define or change their purposes, mandates, accountabilities and authority;
- create, amend or repeal Standing Agreements; and
- propose amendments to this Constitution.

Governance is not a substitute for ordinary operational decision-making.

People exercising relevant organisational roles may participate in governance concerning the structure and authority within which they work.

Board members participate both through the roles they hold and through the responsibilities attached to the Board.

Governance participation may include raising tensions, making proposals, asking questions, expressing reactions, raising objections and participating in integration.

Participation in governance does not give a person a statutory vote or formal authority reserved to the Board or General Assembly.

Where a governance proposal requires a separate formal decision under the Statutes or applicable law, governance may develop and test the proposal, but the relevant statutory body makes the formal decision.

---

## Article 6. Proposals and consent

A governance proposal identifies:

- the tension it is intended to address; and
- the continuing organisational change proposed.

Consent is the normal governance decision principle.

Consent means that a proposal may proceed when no valid objection remains.

Consent does not require:

- unanimous preference;
- unanimous enthusiasm;
- agreement that the proposal is optimal; or
- agreement that everyone would have designed it in the same way.

Concerns, preferences and alternative ideas may improve a proposal without preventing it from proceeding.

A proposal may be adopted through governance only where the participants have authority to make the proposed change.

---

## Article 7. Objections and integration

An objection applies to a proposal, not to the tension that gave rise to it.

A participant has standing to object where:

- the proposal materially affects a role, accountability or authority they hold; or
- the proposal would conflict with this Constitution, the Statutes, applicable law, or an authority that governance does not have the power to change.

Being affected establishes standing. It does not by itself make an objection valid.

A valid objection identifies a concrete harmful effect that adopting the proposal would create within the affected role, accountability or authority, or identifies a conflict with an authority or requirement that governance cannot override.

The objector identifies:

- the harm or conflict;
- how adopting the proposal would cause it; and
- why it is a reason not to proceed rather than a preference or suggestion for improvement.

The objector is not required to design a replacement proposal.

The principle is:

**Scope establishes standing. Concrete harm establishes a valid objection.**

### Board objections

The Board carries broad responsibilities for SDBP as a whole. A proposal may therefore affect a Board responsibility even where the operational work belongs to another role.

This may give a Board member standing to object.

Board membership alone does not make an objection valid.

The Board member must identify the concrete harm, statutory conflict, material risk or impairment of Board responsibility that adopting the proposal would create.

The Board’s general accountability for SDBP does not create an unrestricted right to control work assigned elsewhere.

### Testing and integration

In Quick Consent, the Chair of the Board reviews objections using the validation questions. The Board President is the Chair.

The person raising an objection uses the questions to explain the harm or conflict. The Chair records whether the objection meets the criteria and the reasons for that assessment. The Workspace prompts the Chair to carry out this review; reading the objection does not complete it.

Being the proposer or raising the objection does not by itself create a conflict of interest or prevent the Chair from reviewing it. Actual conflicts of interest remain subject to the applicable rules.

The assessment and its reasons remain visible in the governance record. When the Chair rules an objection invalid, it no longer blocks the proposal. The objector may propose an amendment through a new governance proposal.

In a Governance Meeting, the designated facilitator guides objection testing. The facilitator may rotate. Objection testing identifies potential harm; it is not a vote against the proposal or a choice between preferred solutions.

A valid objection prevents adoption of the proposal through governance until the objection has been resolved through integration.

Integration seeks a version of the proposal that continues to address the original tension while removing or sufficiently changing the harm identified by the objection.

The proposer retains control of the proposal during integration. Other participants may suggest changes, but they do not alter the proposal unless the proposer incorporates them.

A materially amended proposal is again open to objection.

A valid objection does not cease to be valid merely because integration is difficult.

Where the Board or General Assembly has separate statutory authority to make the relevant decision, that body may exercise that authority through its formal decision process. Such a formal decision is distinct from governance consent and does not retrospectively erase an objection raised during governance.

## Article 8. Statutory and reserved authority

The Board and General Assembly retain every responsibility and authority reserved to them by the Statutes or applicable law.

Distributed authority operates within that boundary.

Where a formal Board or General Assembly decision is required, governance does not substitute for it.

No role, governance proposal, consent decision or internal delegation may authorise a person to legally bind SDBP, incur liabilities, spend funds or exercise another reserved power where that authority has not been validly granted.

Authority to explore, communicate, negotiate or develop an opportunity does not by itself include authority to conclude a binding agreement on behalf of SDBP.

Operational authority and authority to create legal or financial commitments are distinct.

The Board may intervene outside the ordinary distributed-authority process where reasonably necessary to:

- fulfil a non-delegable statutory or legal responsibility;
- prevent material legal, financial or organisational harm for which it remains accountable;
- prevent an unauthorised commitment from being created or expanded; or
- respond to serious misuse of organisational authority.

Such intervention is exceptional.

The Board identifies the specific responsibility or harm requiring intervention and uses no more authority than reasonably necessary for that purpose.

Statutory accountability is therefore a boundary and safeguard on distributed authority, not a general mandate for the Board to manage organisational work assigned elsewhere.

---

## Article 9. Organisational governance records

SDBP maintains an accessible record of its current organisational governance, including:

- roles and circles;
- current role assignments;
- current Standing Agreements;
- adopted governance changes; and
- the current Constitution.

Only a governance change that has been adopted through the applicable process changes organisational governance.

Discussion, intention, acknowledgement, informal agreement or software behaviour does not by itself create or change organisational authority.

---

## Article 10. Constitutional stewardship and interpretation

The Chair of the Board safeguards the integrity of this Constitution and supports its proper application.

This stewardship role does not give the Chair authority to take over decisions entrusted to other roles.

The facilitator is responsible for applying the governance process and the objection criteria established by this Constitution.

The role responsible for organisational governance records identifies the current adopted governance and preserves its authoritative wording.

Questions concerning powers or responsibilities reserved by the Statutes or applicable law are determined by the body that holds those responsibilities.

Where constitutional wording remains materially unclear, the appropriate remedy is to clarify the Constitution through governance rather than allow an informal interpretation to create new authority.

---

## Article 11. Adoption, amendment and continuity

This Constitution takes effect only after the formal adoption required under the Statutes has occurred.

Existing valid roles, assignments, agreements and decisions continue after adoption unless they conflict with this Constitution or are subsequently changed through the appropriate authority.

Everyone to whom this Constitution applies may participate in governance concerning proposed amendments to it.

Participation in developing an amendment is distinct from the formal authority required to adopt it.

Where the Statutes require a Board or General Assembly decision, that body takes the formal decision through the applicable statutory process.

The Board’s statutory accountability does not create an unrestricted veto over constitutional governance. Board objections remain subject to the standing and concrete-harm principles of this Constitution, except where the matter concerns an authority or responsibility that cannot legally or statutorily be delegated.

Ordinary changes to roles, circles, assignments, Standing Agreements, working practices or Workspace processes do not require amendment of this Constitution unless they alter the fundamental distribution or limits of organisational authority established here.

If a proposed change requires amendment of the Statutes themselves, the statutory amendment process applies.
